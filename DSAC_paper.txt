Abstract—Industrial Internet of Things enables various
manufacturing processes executed in distributed produc-
tion lines and ﬂexible workshops. With different cloud–
edge–device collaboration ways, interconnected manufac-
turing tasks and computational tasks are cooperatively
completed in manufacturing cells, cloud resources, and
edge resources. Large-scale decision variables and com-
plex precedence constraints make the scheduling problem
intractable. To this end, this article proposed a discretized
soft actor–critic conﬁgured differential evolution algorithm
to ﬁnd a stable solution for the cloud–edge–device collab-
orative task-scheduling problem. A mathematical model is
established to describe the relationship between different
tasks, the variables, the main constraints in collaboration,
and the scheduling targets. A decentralized partially ob-
servable Markov decision process is modeled with ﬁve neu-
ral networks and three discretized loss functions to formu-
late the discretized soft actor–critic policy efﬁciently and
enable it to ﬁnd the best differential evolution conﬁgura-
tions for different scheduling cases. Experimental analysis
of four cloud–edge–device scheduling instances indicates
that the proposed method trained in one case is adaptable
to the other three cases. In the four cases, the proposed
method reduces the total objective by 30.82% and 44.35% at
most compared to ﬁve deep-reinforcement-learning-based
differential evolution algorithms and seven typical evolu-
tionary algorithms, respectively.
Index Terms —Cloud–edge–device collaboration, deep
reinforcement learning (DRL), differential evolution (DE),
Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), task scheduling.

I. I NTRODUCTION
I
NDUSTRIAL Internet of Things (IIoT) closely connects
manufacturing resources, suppliers, products, and customers
with cloud and edge [1]. A general structure of IIoT is shown
in Fig. 1. Cloud data center provides abundant computing re-
sources and storage for industrial data management, multidisci-
plinary knowledge extraction, and complex computation. Edge
resources nearby industrial ﬁelds alleviate the pressure of the
cloud and make real-time supervision and control under the
assistance of the cloud [2].
There are two kinds of tasks to be scheduled in an IIoT-based
manufacturing system: 1) manufacturing tasks and 2) compu-
tational tasks. A manufacturing task deﬁnes the machining or
the assembly process, which can be ﬁnished by a production
line or a workshop. In a general production scenario, each
manufacturing task is further divided into a series of operations.
These operations have strict precedence relationships and can
be executed distributively by different manufacturing devices.
To implement efﬁcient production, each manufacturing task
is usually accompanied by a group of computational appli-
cations, such as machining control, data sampling, numerical
analysis, or digital twin. In traditional manufacturing systems,
these computation applications are deployed on OPC (OLE
(Object Linking and Embedding) for Process Control) servers
(servers in the OLE for Process Control-Uniﬁed Architecture
(OPC-UA) architecture [3]) or Programmable Logic Controller

(PLC) servers locally. In an IIoT platform, they can be combined
as a computational task ofﬂoading to an edge server or a cloud
server. Therefore, a manufacturing task is usually associated
with a computational task.
Consider tasks in Fig.1 as an example, there are six machining
tasks (blue rectangular) producing three subassemblies and one
assembly task (green rectangular) producing a product. Each
machining task corresponds to a digital twin simulation (blue
circle) while the last assembly task is associated with a data
summarization task to predict product quality. When the ﬁrst
digital twin simulation task is initiated, the rest ﬁve digital
twin simulation tasks should be started to collect environmental
data and establish simulation as well. The six digital twin tasks
should be carried out cooperatively and ﬁnished simultaneously
to demonstrate a complete production process of the three
subassemblies. Then, the data summarization task collects data
from the six digital twin simulation tasks and starts assembly
analysis. Obviously, the six digital twin simulation tasks have
a start-time constraint with a precursor start and an end-time
constraint with a precursor ﬁnish while the data summariza-
tion task has a start time constraint with precursor ﬁnish. The
connection relationships between such computational tasks are
complex. The communication overhead among cloud, edge, and
manufacturing resources increases largely [4], [5].
In addition, a computational task requires data from one or
more manufacturing devices and data synchronization with these
devices. If a cloud server executes the task, its input and output
are collected and deployed by an edge server. On the contrary, if
an edge server executes the task, it applies empirical knowledge
and historical data from the cloud data center to implement fast
computing.
With different cloud–edge collaboration modes, numerous
tasks interconnected in complex ways are submitted to the IIoT
platform sporadically. These task requests come from complex
product suppliers for producing components and subassemblies.
As a single complex product (such as an electromobile or au-
tomobile) has hundreds or even thousands of components and
subassemblies, an IIoT platform that connects hundreds of in-
dustrial devices and edge servers can accept at least one hundred
manufacturing tasks at a time. Suppose each task includes ﬁve
basic operations, there are at least 500 variables to determine the
devices for ﬁnishing these manufacturing tasks, 500 variables to
determine the priority of their operations, 100 variables to decide
whether the corresponding computational tasks are ofﬂoaded
to edge or cloud, and 100 variables to ﬁnd the suitable server
for executing the computational tasks. Thousands of variables
make the scheduling of hybrid tasks in the IIoT platform more
challenging [6].
Existing research on task scheduling in the IIoT environment
focuses on either the computational task-scheduling problems
in the cloud–edge environment, or the manufacturing task-
scheduling problems in the industrial ﬁeld [7]. Little research
considered the complex interconnections between these two
sides. The tasks can form a cyclic graph with complex start-
time and end-time constraints. Hence, ﬁnding problem-speciﬁc
heuristics and methods for scheduling scenarios with different
complex precedence constraints is nontrivial.
Therefore, this article establishes a mathematical model of the
hybrid task-scheduling problem in the IIoT environment consid-
ering three task precedence constraints. A deep-reinforcement-
learning (DRL) conﬁgured differential evolution (DE) algorithm
is designed to solve this problem. The evolutionary process is
modeled as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision
process (Dec-POMDP). Then, a discretized soft actor–critic
(DSAC) policy is established for selecting DE mutation opera-
tors. In contrast to other DRL algorithms integrated into the DE
algorithm, the DSAC alleviates the overﬁtting issue and reduces
the instability in the DE algorithm via maximum entropy learn-
ing. Five neural networks and three loss functions are designed
to train the DSAC policy efﬁciently. Experimental analysis of
four cases shows that the proposed DSAC-DE trained in one case
is always adaptable to the other three cases. In the four cases, the
proposed DSAC-DE reduces the overall manufacturing time and
energy consumption by 44.35% and 30.82% at most compared to
seven typical EAs and ﬁve DRL-integrated DE-based (DRL-DE)
algorithms, respectively.
The rest of this article is organized as follows: Section II
presents the recent works on the research of task scheduling in
the IIoT environment. Section III gives the mathematical model
of cloud–edge–device collaborative task scheduling in IIoT.
Section IV introduces the details of the proposed DSAC-DE
algorithm. Experimental results are discussed in Section V.
Finally, Section VI concludes this article.
II. R
ELA TEDWORKS
This section gives a brief overview of the research on task
scheduling in the IIoT environment from two aspects: 1) problem
modeling and 2) method design.
A. Task-Scheduling Models in Cloud–Edge
Collaboration
Computational task scheduling in fog, edge, and cloud com-
puting has been widely studied [8], [9]. The characteristics of
the hierarchical resource connection ways [6], different wireless
transmission costs, and the network function virtualization rules
that inﬂuence the computation and communication efﬁciency are
introduced. Considering the collaboration between cloud and
edge, Xie et al. [10] and Liu et al. [11] scheduled the com-
putational tasks considering the communication of cloud, edge,
and manufacturing devices. Nevertheless, the inﬂuence between
manufacturing tasks and computational tasks is ignored.
In recent years, manufacturing task scheduling in ﬂexible and
distributed workshops has attracted considerable interest [12],
[13]. The main target is to realize just-in-time manufacturing
with due-time constraints [14]. The issues about production
line reconﬁguration, dynamic resource occupancy, and logistics
among distributed workshops that inﬂuence the manufacturing
efﬁciency are taken into consideration [15], [16], [17].V a r i -
ous task precedence constraints under different production pro-
cesses, including sequence, parallel, and loop, are analyzed[17].
However, the collaboration of cloud and edge for manufacturing
tasks is not considered. The inﬂuence between manufacturing
tasks and the related computational tasks is neglected.

B. Task-Scheduling Algorithms in the IIoT Environment
Commonly applied scheduling algorithms include determin-
istic mathematical programming methods, EAs, and DRL. Due
to the high complexity, deterministic methods are usually ap-
plied as heuristics for small-scale local search [6]. EAs with
population-based iteration are more adaptable for solving dif-
ferent task-scheduling problems [13], [15]. Y et the extensibility
of a ﬁxed EA for different scheduling scenarios is limited.
To solve dynamic scheduling problems, DRL is introduced to
learn policies under dynamic contexts and generate scheduling
solutions [12]. However, DRL requires a long training process
to obtain similar accuracy compared with classical EAs [18].
Recent research also applies DRL as a hyperheuristic to adjust
EAs in solving task-scheduling problems [19]. Typical studies
include the use of deep Q-learning network (DQN) and actor-
critic (AC) models to adjust hyperparameters of GAs, variable
neighborhood search, particle swarm optimization (PSO), DEs,
and so forth [20], [21], [22]. Based on these studies, Zhang
et al. [23] adopted a radial basis network as a surrogate to
expand the dataset of DQN. Sharma et al. [24] deﬁned complex
state representation with 99 variables and three different reward
functions to train the DQN. Xu et al. [25] deﬁned the reward
as the improvement of the current state and the next state
during the evolutionary search. However, all of these works
model the evolutionary process as a Markov decision process
(MDP) without random noise. However, the randomness in the
evolutionary search process always makes the training of DRL
nontrivial, but none of these works discussed this issue.

To explore whether the performance of a trained DSAC policy
will decline when the resource availability changes, the four

suitable scheduling solutions for different scenarios with only
one-time training.
VI. C
ONCLUSION
This article studied the cloud–edge–device collaborative task-
scheduling problem with three kinds of task constraints. To solve
this problem in a parameter-free way, a DSAC-DE algorithm
was proposed. The evolutionary process was modeled as a
Dec-POMDP . The maximum entropy learning in DSAC reduced
the inﬂuence of searching randomness of DE and alleviated
overﬁtting. Experimental results veriﬁed the effectiveness of
the proposed algorithm in different scales of cloud–edge–device
collaborative task-scheduling problems and proved its superior-
ity compared to other typical EAs and DRL-based EAs.
Future works include developing an end-to-end perception of
the scheduling scene to generate an adaptable state and reward
and designing parallel strategies to accelerate both the training
and searching process.
